ADVISE: send-back

1. **Specify signing-key validity in the receipt contract.** [design.md:30](/Users/dukejones/work/WorldTree/Conduit/openspec/changes/add-pdu-wire/design.md:30) leaves lookup semantics undefined. Current [pipeline.rs:255](/Users/dukejones/work/WorldTree/Conduit/conduit-server/src/federation/pipeline.rs:255) obtains bare key bytes; [remote_keys.rs:166](/Users/dukejones/work/WorldTree/Conduit/conduit-server/src/remote_keys.rs:166) checks wall-clock cache expiry, then returns freshly fetched keys without an event-timestamp check. Require event-aware lookup, the seven-day validity cap, and rejection when no eligible signature remains. Test timestamps at and beyond expiry, including historical events after key rotation. [Matrix v11 signing-key validity](https://spec.matrix.org/v1.19/rooms/v11/#signing-key-validity-period).

2. **Require the authorising server’s signature on restricted-join events.** Current [signing.rs:299](/Users/dukejones/work/WorldTree/Conduit/conduit/src/signing.rs:299) returns success immediately after verifying the sender; other servers’ signatures are optional. The proposed `check_received` preserves this unspecified policy. When `join_authorised_via_users_server` is present, verify that user’s homeserver signature before admission, including fetched auth events. Add sender-only, invalid-authoriser, and valid-co-signed vectors. This is a signature check independent of deferred recursive authorization. [Matrix v11 authorization rule 4.2.1](https://spec.matrix.org/v1.19/rooms/v11/#authorisation-rules).

3. **Make legacy integrity checks unavoidable on outbound paths.** [design.md:219](/Users/dukejones/work/WorldTree/Conduit/openspec/changes/add-pdu-wire/design.md:219) permits direct `.pdu` emission; [design.md:228](/Users/dukejones/work/WorldTree/Conduit/openspec/changes/add-pdu-wire/design.md:228) converts legacy queue snapshots by removing `event_id`. Neither necessarily invokes the later `get_wire_pdu` gate. Current [queue.rs:293](/Users/dukejones/work/WorldTree/Conduit/conduit-server/src/federation/queue.rs:293) sends snapshots directly. Removing metadata cannot repair old hashes or signatures. Require classification against the saved local ID before legacy emission, withholding mismatches without re-signing them into a new identity. Test a pending pre-`56498e3` envelope. [Matrix reference hashing](https://spec.matrix.org/v1.19/server-server-api/#calculating-the-reference-hash-for-an-event).

4. **Resolve the legacy backfill’s effect on client history.** [design.md:333](/Users/dukejones/work/WorldTree/Conduit/openspec/changes/add-pdu-wire/design.md:333) backfills redacted `pdu_json`, while the universal decoder reads it whenever present ([design.md:306](/Users/dukejones/work/WorldTree/Conduit/openspec/changes/add-pdu-wire/design.md:306)). Consequently, classifying a lossy legacy message removes its body from client reads, contradicting “client-facing reads are unchanged” at line 367. Define separate retained-history and federation-serving behavior, or explicitly specify the migration’s client-visible redaction. Test client reads before and after both inline classification and the sweep. Federation can serve a calculated redaction without silently replacing retained history. [Matrix hash-mismatch handling](https://spec.matrix.org/v1.19/server-server-api/#validating-hashes-and-signatures-on-received-events).

5. **Correct the migration’s lock assumptions.** [design.md:240](/Users/dukejones/work/WorldTree/Conduit/openspec/changes/add-pdu-wire/design.md:240) combines metadata-only `events` alterations with table rewrites in one transaction. Its `events` exclusive lock lasts through those rewrites. Moreover, `transactions` has no implemented retention/GC, so “bounded caches” does not establish a short duration. Separate the event-column additions from rewrites and specify measured downtime or a staged conversion strategy. PostgreSQL documents both [rewrite costs](https://www.postgresql.org/docs/16/sql-altertable.html#SQL-ALTERTABLE-NOTES) and [locks retained until transaction end](https://www.postgresql.org/docs/16/explicit-locking.html#LOCKING-TABLES).
